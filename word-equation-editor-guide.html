Microsoft Word equation editor: how to insert mathematical equations in Word

Even if you use a converter, it helps to know the Microsoft Word equation editor. Small fixes are much quicker when you know where things are.

How to insert mathematical equations in Word

Open the Insert tab and click Equation, or simply press Alt + = on your keyboard. A box appears in the document and a new Equation tab opens with ready-made structures: fractions, roots, integrals, brackets and matrices. Click one and fill in the empty slots with Tab.

Word equation shortcuts

Inside an equation box you can type short codes and press the space bar to turn them into symbols:

Word mathematical symbols

If you can't remember a code, look at the Symbols group on the Equation tab. It shows Greek letters, operators and arrows, and you can click to insert any of them. Frequently used ones are worth learning by code, because typing is faster than hunting through menus.

Complex equations in Word

Long formulas such as Fourier series, matrices or multi-line derivations are painful to build by hand. A better approach is to copy complex equations into Word from the source. Paste the AI answer into MathToWord, check the preview, and download the Word file. You get the full formula as a real equation and can edit it afterwards with the tools above.

Fixing a formula that looks too tall or too wide

Click the equation, open the Equation tab and choose Professional or Linear in the Conversions group. Professional stacks fractions neatly. Linear keeps everything on one line, which is handy inside a sentence.
