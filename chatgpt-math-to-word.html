ChatGPT equations to Word: why they break and how to fix them

Most students who use ChatGPT for maths run into the same annoying moment. The answer looks perfect in the chat. You paste it into Word and get something like \frac{a}{b}, or boxes, or a line of squashed symbols. Nothing is wrong with your computer. The two programs simply speak different languages.

What is going on behind the screen

ChatGPT, Gemini and DeepSeek don't send you a picture of a formula. They send LaTeX, which is a text code for maths. A web library on the page reads that code and draws the fraction or the integral. When you copy, you either get the raw code or a styled piece of web page.

What Word expects

Word has its own equation format. Inside, it stores each formula in a structure called OMML. It can't turn a styled web page into that structure, and it only understands LaTeX when you paste it into an equation box yourself. That's why the same answer behaves differently depending on how you copy it.

How MathToWord bridges the gap

The tool reads the LaTeX, shows you a preview so you can check it, and then writes the formulas in Word's own format. It does this in your browser. If you download the Word file, you can open it and click any equation to edit it. Headings, bold text, lists and tables from the AI answer come along too.

How to convert AI math output to Word

  1. Click the Copy button under the ChatGPT reply.
  2. Paste it into the box on the home page.
  3. Check the preview.
  4. Click Download Word file and open it.

Does it work for other AI tools?

Yes. AI equations to Word is the same problem everywhere, because Claude, Copilot, Grok and the rest also write LaTeX. If a tool uses unusual brackets, the preview will show you straight away whether the equation was found.

Before you hand it in

A converter fixes the format, not the maths. Work through at least the important steps yourself, since AI sometimes makes small mistakes that look convincing.
